# Data Handling Essentials

## Course summary

| Field      | Design                                                                                               |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience   | All employees and contractors                                                                        |
| Duration   | 22 minutes                                                                                           |
| Goal       | Help learners classify, minimise, protect, share, retain, and report work information appropriately. |
| Completion | Complete six module checks and score at least 7/8 in the final assessment.                           |
| Tone       | Clear, practical, policy-aware, and focused on safe daily decisions.                                 |

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, learners can:

1. Recognise common information categories and their handling implications.
2. Use the minimum information needed for a task.
3. Choose approved storage, sharing, and collaboration methods.
4. Handle a suspected data incident quickly and responsibly.

## Module structure

### Module 1 — Information is an asset

- **1.1 Why information needs care:** Data can create value and risk.
- **1.2 Common categories:** Public, internal, confidential, personal, regulated, and highly restricted.
- **1.3 Context matters:** A harmless fact can become sensitive when combined with other information.
- **1.4 Ownership and accountability:** Everyone has a role in handling data safely.
- **Knowledge check:** Which item needs the most careful handling in a typical work context?
- **Media direction:** Side image of a protected laptop and information categories overlay.

### Module 2 — Classify before you share

- **2.1 Start with the data-classification policy:** Use policy labels and approved guidance.
- **2.2 Personal data:** Customer, employee, candidate, and contact details.
- **2.3 Confidential business information:** Contracts, pricing, roadmaps, and source code.
- **2.4 Regulated and special-category data:** Escalate uncertainty rather than guessing.
- **Knowledge check:** Choose the correct first step before sharing a customer spreadsheet.
- **Interaction:** Clickable classification cards with text descriptions and a keyboard alternative.
- **Media direction:** Wide classification matrix with no real personal information.

### Module 3 — Minimise and protect

- **3.1 Minimum necessary:** Use only the content required to complete a task.
- **3.2 Redaction and anonymisation:** They reduce risk but are not automatic approval.
- **3.3 Secure devices and workspaces:** Lock screens and protect printed material.
- **3.4 Passwords, keys, and secrets:** Never place them in unapproved files, tools, or chats.
- **Knowledge check:** Identify the safer version of a document to use in a collaboration task.
- **Media direction:** Side image of a lock and laptop with a restrained, professional visual treatment.

### Module 4 — Store and share safely

- **4.1 Approved locations:** Use the right repository, account, and access group.
- **4.2 Access control:** Give access only to people with a legitimate need.
- **4.3 External sharing:** Confirm recipients, permissions, expiry, and the approved transfer method.
- **4.4 Collaboration tools and AI:** Check the specific tool, account, feature, and data type are permitted.
- **Knowledge check:** Choose the safest way to send a confidential file to an approved external recipient.
- **Interaction:** Scenario with clearly labelled, keyboard-selectable sharing options.
- **Media direction:** Wide secure-sharing journey from owner to authorised recipient.

### Module 5 — Retain, dispose, and report

- **5.1 Retention:** Keep information only as long as policy or law requires.
- **5.2 Disposal:** Use approved deletion and destruction processes.
- **5.3 Mistakes happen:** Misaddressed messages and incorrect permissions need prompt action.
- **5.4 Incident reporting:** Report facts quickly; do not conceal or perform unapproved cleanup.
- **Knowledge check:** Put the correct response steps in order after sharing a file with the wrong recipient.
- **Media direction:** Side image of a secure archive and incident-report pathway.

### Module 6 — Make safe handling routine

- **6.1 Pause before sending:** Check content, recipients, and permissions.
- **6.2 Ask when unsure:** Policy owners and security teams are there to help.
- **6.3 Protect the organisation and people:** Small habits prevent large incidents.
- **6.4 Personal commitment:** Name one task where you will apply the minimum-necessary principle.
- **Knowledge check:** Select the best response to an unclear data-handling request.
- **Media direction:** Wide summary graphic: classify, minimise, protect, share, report.

## Final assessment

- Eight practical decisions: classification, personal data, permissions, secure storage, external sharing, AI use, retention, and incident reporting.
- Pass mark: 7/8.
- Give feedback that explains the decision and directs learners to policy support where required.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Never use sample records that resemble real employees, customers, account numbers, or contracts.
- Every classification or ordering interaction needs a button-based, screen-reader-friendly fallback.
- Pair visual classification labels with plain language; do not communicate sensitivity through colour alone.
- Keep visuals contextual but non-sensitive, and respect reduced-motion preferences.
