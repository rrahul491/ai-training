# Recognising Phishing

## Course summary

| Field      | Design                                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience   | All employees and contractors                                                                                          |
| Duration   | 18 minutes                                                                                                             |
| Goal       | Help learners identify suspicious messages and report them safely before an account, payment, or data incident occurs. |
| Completion | Complete six module checks and score at least 7/8 in the final assessment.                                             |
| Tone       | Calm, practical, non-blaming, and action-oriented.                                                                     |

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, learners can:

1. Identify common social-engineering techniques.
2. Inspect a message, link, attachment, sender, and request before acting.
3. Recognise urgent payment, credential, MFA, and impersonation risks.
4. Report a suspicious message without forwarding or interacting with it.

## Module structure

### Module 1 — Why phishing works

- **1.1 The goal of a phishing attempt:** Steal access, money, data, or trust.
- **1.2 Social engineering:** Urgency, authority, curiosity, and fear.
- **1.3 Common channels:** Email, text, messaging apps, voice, QR codes, and social media.
- **1.4 Your pause point:** Slow down when a request changes normal process.
- **Knowledge check:** A message claims an account will close in ten minutes. What should you do first?
- **Media direction:** Side image of a realistic-but-generic inbox; highlight no real company brands or personal details.

### Module 2 — Inspect the message

- **2.1 Sender names and addresses:** Display names can be misleading.
- **2.2 Links:** Preview the destination before opening it.
- **2.3 Attachments:** Treat unexpected files and macros with caution.
- **2.4 Writing clues:** Errors matter, but polished writing can still be malicious.
- **Knowledge check:** Select the strongest evidence that a message needs further review.
- **Interaction:** Click-to-reveal message anatomy, with keyboard-operable hotspots and a text equivalent.
- **Media direction:** Wide annotated email mock-up.

### Module 3 — Recognise high-risk requests

- **3.1 Credential and MFA prompts:** Never share passwords or authentication codes.
- **3.2 Payment and bank-detail changes:** Verify through a known, independent channel.
- **3.3 Executive and supplier impersonation:** Confirm unusual requests outside the message thread.
- **3.4 Data requests:** Follow classification and approved-sharing rules.
- **Knowledge check:** A supplier asks to change bank details. Identify the safe verification step.
- **Media direction:** Side image of a secure verification workflow; avoid real financial data.

### Module 4 — Act safely

- **4.1 Do not click, reply, or forward:** Preserve the original message.
- **4.2 Use the reporting channel:** Make reporting visible and simple.
- **4.3 If you interacted:** Disconnect only when instructed and report promptly.
- **4.4 Reporting is helpful:** Early reports protect colleagues.
- **Knowledge check:** Order the correct actions after selecting a suspicious link.
- **Interaction:** Accessible ordered-choice scenario; buttons rather than drag-and-drop only.
- **Media direction:** Wide step-by-step report-and-recover timeline.

### Module 5 — Phishing beyond email

- **5.1 Smishing and messaging scams:** Treat unexpected messages as untrusted.
- **5.2 Vishing and deepfake calls:** Verify a caller through a trusted directory or callback number.
- **5.3 QR and shared-document lures:** Check the destination before signing in.
- **5.4 Social-media requests:** Keep work and personal boundaries clear.
- **Knowledge check:** Choose the safest response to an unexpected voice request from an executive.
- **Media direction:** Side collage of generic phone, chat, QR, and video-call indicators.

### Module 6 — Build a reporting habit

- **6.1 The three-step habit:** Pause, inspect, report.
- **6.2 Personal accountability:** You are not expected to investigate alone.
- **6.3 Team protection:** One report can prevent many incidents.
- **6.4 Personal action plan:** Know your organisation’s reporting route before you need it.
- **Knowledge check:** Select the best response when you are unsure whether a message is malicious.
- **Media direction:** Wide summary graphic with the three-step habit.

## Final assessment

- Eight scenario-based questions.
- Pass mark: 7/8.
- Include at least one scenario each for email, payment change, MFA, text message, voice call, attachment, and reporting.
- Incorrect answers provide explanation and permit a retry.

## Accessibility and implementation notes

- Do not reproduce harmful live links, QR codes, phone numbers, or personal data.
- Hotspots require equivalent labelled buttons and descriptive text in the DOM.
- Avoid relying on red flags shown only by colour; pair every signal with text or an icon label.
- Preserve keyboard order, visible focus, reduced-motion support, narration controls, and local resume state.
